About the group lead

Dr Drupad Trivedi

Senior Lecturer in Analytical, Measurements and Physical Chemistry at the University of Manchester, and Chair in Data Analytics for the Community for Analytical Measurement Science. He is associated with the Christabel Pankhurst Institute, the Manchester Institute of Biotechnology and Digital Futures.

Drupad received his BSc (Hons) in Biomedical Sciences and his PhD in biomarker discovery using metabolomics from Middlesex University. He briefly trained as a postdoctoral fellow at the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine, then worked at the University of Manchester with Prof Royston Goodacre and Prof Perdita Barran before starting independent research.

His expertise is in global metabolomics analysis of biological systems, combined with data-driven analysis. His interests include personalised medicine and biomarker discovery from body fluids, and projects on Parkinson's disease, rare tropical diseases, asthma, lung cancer and the detection of prohibited substances.

Research

Our research interests are applications of chemometrics in clinical metabolomics and identifying the molecular targets that wearable sensing needs for disease diagnostics. The group is currently working on rapid diagnosis of tuberculosis.

What we have found

Our aim is to turn the molecules that bodies give off, on the skin, in breath, in saliva and in blood, into diagnostic tests that are fast, cheap and easy to collect. The group designs the sampling, builds the mass spectrometry and chemometrics workflows, and then tests the resulting models on people and samples they have never seen. This work has contributed to the evidence that skin sebum carries a measurable signature of Parkinson's disease, led to metabolite signals that separate antibiotic resistant bacteria within hours, and produced population scale studies of ageing and frailty in serum from cohorts of more than 500 and more than 1,000 people. It draws on collaborations across Europe, Africa, North America, the Middle East and India, with partners in microbiology, neurology, respiratory medicine and agrochemical science. The main themes are below, with what each study found.

Breath and lung health

3 papers

A review of breath metabolomics set out how patterns of volatile organic compounds in exhaled breath can be captured, identified and quantified to find disease biomarkers and judge prognosis (Trends in Biotechnology, 2014). Human neutrophils exposed to electronic cigarette vapour extract raised their surface CD11b and CD66b, released more MMP-9 and CXCL8, showed higher neutrophil elastase and MMP-9 activity, and activated p38 MAPK. Mass spectrometry also found harmful chemicals in the extract, including known carcinogens, which raises concern over the safety of e-cigarettes (Respiratory Research, 2016).

More recently, in the ExPeL study, exhaled breath condensate was collected from people in the UK Targeted Lung Health Check programme. Principal component analysis, linear discriminant analysis and Mahalanobis distance separated cancer from control participants. Class imbalance was handled with SMOTE, and a voting ensemble reached 85.7% accuracy, an AUC of 0.90 and no false positives on held-out data. With only 34 evaluable participants, this is an encouraging early result that needs a larger cohort (Clinical and Translational Science, 2026).

Metabolomics for personalised medicine

5 papers

Early work argued that urine needs its own analytical choices. Urine is highly polar and suits hydrophilic interaction chromatography, and the natural variability of metabolite profiles has to be established in large sets of normal controls before clinical baselines mean anything (Biomedical Chromatography, 2014). That thinking was then applied to pregnancy. Hydrophilic interaction LC-MS of 72 urine samples from 9 to 23 weeks of gestation mapped 383 ions and found that four fell sharply between the first and second trimesters (Biomedical Chromatography, 2015). In 122 maternal urine samples from women with and without an aneuploid pregnancy, mostly Down syndrome, hydrophilic interaction chromatography resolved 10 times more unique ions than reversed phase. Two of them, identified as progesterone and dihydrouracil, changed in step with the presence of a fetus with Down syndrome (Biomedical Chromatography, 2015).

Two reviews set out the wider case. One argued that clinical practice measures only a handful of biochemicals, while metabolomics can describe far more of a patient and so guide interventions, provided the barriers to clinical translation are met (New Horizons in Translational Medicine, 2017). The other covered clinical lipidomics from sample collection and preparation through to data interpretation, with an appraisal of applications in 2022 (Analytical Science Advances, 2023).

Microbial and cell metabolism

11 papers

Two Nature papers in 2015 explained how a family of bacterial decarboxylases works. UbiX is a flavin prenyltransferase that makes a new prenylated flavin cofactor, which UbiD needs for ubiquinone biosynthesis (Nature, 2015). In the companion study, the fungal enzyme Fdc1 was shown to be solely responsible for reversible decarboxylation using that cofactor, and crystal structures led to the proposal of the first enzymatic 1,3-dipolar cycloaddition (Nature, 2015).

The group has also used metabolomics to study microbes as production hosts. In Escherichia coli grown anaerobically on glycerol, the reductive branch of the TCA cycle was the most relevant pathway for hydrogen and ethanol production, with malate playing a central role (Biotechnology Journal, 2015). The metabolic burden of making recombinant proteins was measured in Streptomyces lividans producing mouse tumour necrosis factor alpha, where infrared spectra separated the wild type from the empty plasmid and producing strains (Microbial Cell Factories, 2015), and in E. coli producing green fluorescent protein, where induction with IPTG was the dominant factor hampering growth and metabolism (Molecular BioSystems, 2016).

Studies of Pseudomonas putida DOT-T1E followed how the cells respond to toluene and to magnesium and calcium ions (Metabolites, 2016), and how toluene changes their metabolism using infrared spectroscopy and GC-MS (Metabolomics, 2016). With propranolol, the efflux pump TtgABC mattered more than TtgGHI for tolerance, and ornithine took part in the response (PLOS ONE, 2016). Infrared screening of microalgal mutants distinguished a number of them from wild type under nutrient limitation (Metabolomics, 2016), and two glycerol-3-phosphate dehydrogenases from a microalga were found to have distinct roles in lipid metabolism, with knockdowns lowering triacylglycerol under phosphorus or nitrogen starvation (Plant Physiology, 2017). In Chinese hamster ovary cells, silencing each TCA cycle gene in turn showed that silencing at least four was detrimental to growth, and mitochondrial aconitase emerged as a key regulator of energy production for growth and protein expression (Metabolomics, 2018).

Rapid analytical fingerprinting

6 papers

These studies used rapid physicochemical techniques to fingerprint microbes, drugs and food. Infrared and Raman spectroscopy and MALDI-TOF mass spectrometry each separated 11 Campylobacter strains from six species, including two very closely related subspecies of C. fetus, generally in agreement with 16S rRNA phylogeny (Analyst, 2016). For 33 strains of Bacillus and Brevibacillus, lipid profiles classified seven species correctly on average 62.23% of the time by MALDI-TOF-MS and 77.03% by LC-MS (Analytical and Bioanalytical Chemistry, 2016). A fractional factorial design was used to optimise MALDI-TOF-MS sample preparation for four phospholipids and one acylglycerol in complex mixtures (Analytical Chemistry, 2016).

Surface-enhanced Raman scattering was used to detect and quantify propranolol spiked into human serum, plasma and urine at physiologically relevant concentrations (Analytical Chemistry, 2016), and was coupled online to gradient liquid chromatography to quantify methotrexate and two of its metabolites, including in urine from patients on the drug (Analytical Chemistry, 2017). Beyond the clinic, GC-MS and UHPLC-MS profiling of beef and pork mince mixed in different ratios gave a panel of metabolites for detecting the adulteration of beef with pork (Analyst, 2016).

Parkinson's disease from the skin

9 papers

The work began with a person who could smell Parkinson's disease on others. Sebum swabbed from the upper back was analysed by thermal desorption gas chromatography mass spectrometry, and a volatile signature of disease emerged, including altered perillic aldehyde and eicosane (ACS Central Science, 2019). A larger replication with 129 people classified 84.4% of cases correctly using supervised multivariate models, with variable importance in projection scores used to pick out the features that mattered. It also showed that some earlier annotations were probably breakdown products of lipids, which is a useful warning for the field (ACS Central Science, 2021).

Liquid chromatography mass spectrometry of 274 sebum samples then pointed to disturbed lipid metabolism, in the carnitine shuttle, sphingolipids, arachidonic acid and fatty acid biosynthesis (Nature Communications, 2021). Paper spray ion mobility mass spectrometry cut analysis to three minutes per swab across 150 people, and flagged triglycerides and larger acyl glycerides as diagnostic (JACS Au, 2022). In people with isolated REM sleep behaviour disorder, an early warning state, 55 volatile features sat between those of controls and Parkinson's, and two of three samples that smelled like Parkinson's belonged to people who later showed symptoms (npj Parkinson's Disease, 2025). Trained dogs detected the odour with sensitivity of 70 to 80% and specificity of 90 to 98% (Journal of Parkinson's Disease, 2025).

Method studies matter too. Sebum samples could not be told apart by storage temperature or time for up to four weeks, which suggests that sampling at home and posting samples is feasible (Scientific Reports, 2024). Serum metabolites and gut bacteria in 124 people also linked altered lipid and energy metabolism to the gut microbiome in Parkinson's (npj Parkinson's Disease, 2022).

Healthy ageing

2 papers

Serum from 1,191 older adults, with longitudinal validation in 786, showed that frailty is associated with disturbed vitamin E and carnitine pathways. Twelve metabolites, including three tocotrienols and six carnitines, separated frail from non-frail people (Nature Communications, 2019). In 572 men of White European, South Asian and African-Caribbean ethnicity, PLS-DA models validated by bootstrap and permutation testing classified ethnicity with 90.5% accuracy for LC-MS data and 85.6% for GC-MS data, a reminder that ethnicity needs to be considered when setting metabolic reference ranges (Metabolomics, 2024).

Infection and COVID-19

3 papers

Saliva from hospital patients was analysed by LC-MS, and a partial least squares discriminant analysis model using six features, five of them amino acids, agreed fully with the clinical severity call for COVID-19 (PLoS ONE, 2022). In a pilot of 67 patients, sebum lipids were lower in those with COVID-19, particularly triglycerides and ceramides. Classification improved from 57% sensitivity and 68% specificity to 79% and 83% once comorbidities were controlled for (EClinicalMedicine, 2021). In Uganda, among 209 people, Schistosoma mansoni infection was linked to higher gut microbial diversity and distinct metabolites, and several microbes statistically mediated the link to lower cardiovascular risk. The design was cross-sectional, so cause cannot be inferred (Nature Communications, 2026).

Skin biology and agrochemicals

3 papers

Over 24 hours in eight healthy volunteers, cosinor analysis found only 29 skin lipids (0.67%) with a significant circadian rhythm, and none survived a stricter false discovery rate and Lomb-Scargle analysis. PCA and PLS-DA showed no clustering by time, so time of sampling has a negligible effect on average (The Analyst, 2025). Mass spectrometry imaging has also been used to follow where a fungicide moves in plants, through the xylem and across the leaf in tomato (The Analyst, 2024) and through the wax and tissue of wheat leaves (Analytical and Bioanalytical Chemistry, 2025).

Rapid detection of antibiotic resistance

2 papers

Culture-based tests for carbapenem resistance need long incubations. Using LC-MS profiling of 32 Klebsiella pneumoniae and Escherichia coli isolates after only six hours of growth, partial least squares discriminant analysis, k-nearest neighbour and random forest models identified 21 metabolite biomarkers that predicted resistance, all with AUROC values of at least 0.845 (Metabolomics, 2025). A volatile approach found that the ratio of two leucine-derived compounds, 3-methyl-1-butanol and 3-methylbutanal, classified resistance with 100% sensitivity and 94.1% specificity across 16 isolates. The ratio tracked minimum inhibitory concentration, and isotope tracing confirmed that both come from leucine breakdown (Microbiology Spectrum, 2026). These are small isolate sets, so larger clinical validation is the next step.
